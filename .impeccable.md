## Design Context

### Users
The portfolio serves three audiences at once: hiring managers evaluating fit quickly, design peers looking for craft and taste, and freelance clients deciding whether the work feels trustworthy enough to start a conversation. Visitors usually scan first, then dip into projects, resume details, or contact links when something resonates.

### Brand Personality
Quiet, minimal, aesthetic. The interface should feel calm, precise, tactile, and edited rather than flashy. It should suggest strong visual judgment without asking for attention too loudly.

### Aesthetic Direction
Warm minimal portfolio with a single Aqua / early-2000s soft-plastic contact artifact: glossy translucent bevels, pale gray fill, blue-gray rim light, and heavy soft shadow, surrounded by otherwise quiet paper-like structure. The site should feel personal and designed, but not decorative for its own sake. It should explicitly not feel loud, busy, neon, over-animated, or generically "AI-polished."

### Design Principles
- Keep the first read calm: high signal, low noise, no loud copy or decorative filler.
- Make interactive surfaces feel tactile with subtle depth, precise spacing, and fast restrained motion.
- Use accent color sparingly so contact, resume, and project actions stay intentional.
- Prefer edited personal details over broad marketing language.
- Validate visual changes in the browser at desktop and mobile sizes before calling them done.

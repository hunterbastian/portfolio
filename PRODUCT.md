# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The portfolio serves three audiences in priority order:

1. Hiring managers and recruiters who need to evaluate craft, reliability, taste, and fit quickly.
2. Freelance clients who want enough trust and signal to start a project conversation.
3. Design-minded builders and peers who look for interaction detail, visual judgment, and technical care.

Most visitors scan first. They should be able to understand who Hunter is, what kind of work he makes, and where to go next without reading every case study. The strongest visitors then dip into projects, resume details, Playground experiments, or contact links when something resonates.

## Product Purpose

The site is a personal portfolio for Hunter Bastian, a design engineer and interaction design student based in Utah. It exists to show calm interface craft, thoughtful motion, visual restraint, and credible product judgment through the site itself and the selected project work.

Success means the site feels designed, current, and personal without becoming decorative or loud. It should make a recruiter comfortable moving Hunter forward, make a client comfortable reaching out, and make a peer notice that the details were intentionally built.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, tactile.

The voice should feel edited and human: confident without hype, personal without oversharing, and specific without sounding like a template. The visual tone should feel warm, minimal, lightly material, and carefully composed. It should suggest strong visual judgment by showing restraint, not by explaining taste.

## Anti-references

Do not let the portfolio feel like:

- A generic SaaS landing page with metric blocks, stock gradients, and conversion-section scaffolding.
- A generic agency portfolio with oversized case-study cards, interchangeable project blurbs, and decorative section wrappers.
- An over-glossy glass UI where blur and shine become the whole identity.
- A loud neon or dark-purple AI-generated interface.
- A cute, toy-like portfolio where charm weakens professional trust.
- A blank minimalist site that hides the work and personality behind too much emptiness.
- A repeated tiny-uppercase eyebrow system on every section.
- A page that depends on sound, haptics, or motion to be understandable.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the first read calm. The site should communicate quickly with high signal, low noise, and no decorative filler.
2. Let the work carry the proof. Project imagery, interaction details, and implementation quality should do more than explanatory copy.
3. Make surfaces feel tactile, not glossy by default. Depth, blur, shadow, and shine need a named job such as launchpad depth, hover feedback, or hero atmosphere.
4. Prefer edited personal details over marketing language. Specific project context beats broad claims.
5. Preserve the portfolio as the strongest case study. Every page should demonstrate the same craft standards the projects claim.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for text contrast, focus visibility, keyboard access, and touch target sizing. Reduced motion must be respected, and motion should never be the only way to understand state. Sound and haptics are optional enhancements only. Interactive controls should keep at least a 40px hit target, with 44px preferred for isolated mobile icon controls.
